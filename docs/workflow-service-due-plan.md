# Repeat service reminders

Status: implementation integrated locally; verification recorded below, November 22.

## First slice

Notify a selected teammate when a customer is due for a selected service again. Use an explicit 3-, 6- or 12-calendar-month interval after the latest recorded completion of a Job for that customer/service. The interval belongs to the reviewed workflow definition, keyed to the existing stable workspace service ID. One active rule per service avoids competing schedules. No extra Services settings field or second source of scheduling truth is needed for this slice.

This is elapsed-time recurrence, not a calendar-season campaign. Annual calendar-date reviews now use the separate [annual service review recipe](workflow-seasonal-service-plan.md). “Every spring” asks for a custom date and is not silently translated to “every six months.” Customer-requested deferred-work dates, automatic customer communication, consent/preferences for marketing and per-property schedules are separate later slices.

## Completion evidence

Jobs currently record status changes in Job activity; there is no distinct editable actual service-completed date. Use the timestamp of an authoritative transition to Completed, labeled “Marked completed” in the review. Never substitute job creation, scheduled visit, last edited date, or a completed Work Order for completion of the whole Job. A Job without a customer, recognized service or reliable completion event does not enroll. Explain that limit when historical data cannot establish a due date.

Record a completion cycle/version durably with the workflow occurrence. Multiple completed Jobs for the same customer/service use the latest valid completion, with a deterministic tie break. Reopening or canceling that source invalidates its cycle; a new completion creates a new cycle. Recompute from current valid facts rather than adding months again to a previous reminder. Calendar-month addition clamps to the last valid day (for example, August 31 plus six months), in the saved workspace timezone.

The first version groups at customer/service level. It does not identify properties from free-text work locations or treat different addresses as separate service contracts. The review list links to the source Job so users can check the actual work/location before contacting anyone.

## Simple user flow

1. Ask on Workflows: “Remind me six months after a customer's last completed gutter cleaning.”
2. The assistant gathers only missing service, interval, notification contact and send time. The compact review states: “Notify Morgan at 9 AM when customers are due for gutter cleaning, six months after their latest recorded completion.” Show timezone and In-app/Email, both on by default. Duration is a calendar interval; no weekday/business-day inference.
3. Publication applies to future Job completions by default. An explicit “Include existing completed jobs” choice offers a bounded reviewed initialization of reliable current completion history. Do not enroll historical customers or send an overdue backlog without that reviewed choice. Initialize in bounded background batches and expose progress/limits; publication never waits for an unbounded historical scan.
4. On each due date, notify the team once for the newly due customers: “3 customers are due for gutter cleaning.” A single Review customers action opens a protected live due list for that workflow. If a selected send time has passed today, the review states the next delivery time; do not silently produce a late backlog of notifications.
5. Use the shared compact table and global pagination on the due list: Customer, Last marked completed, Due date, Actions. Keep contact information on the existing customer page. Actions: Open customer, Open source job, Remind me on a selected future date, Dismiss this cycle. Dismiss writes only the current reminder resolution; it never claims the customer was contacted, sends a message, or changes the customer/Job. Snooze retains the completion-based original due date and the separately selected next reminder date.
6. The workflow menu includes View due customers so the list remains reachable after its notification is read. Existing Edit, Pause, Resume and typed-confirm Delete apply. Edit is reviewed; it never silently replays resolved cycles. No Test step in creation/publication.

## Suppression and notification behavior

- Skip archived/unavailable customers, archived services and sources no longer completed. Skip current eligible Jobs already scheduled for that customer and the same service; unrelated work does not suppress a reminder.
- Avoid prompting creation of duplicate work: an existing active unscheduled Job for the customer/service is not another “customer due” opportunity. Surface its existing Job link in the due list as “Work already open,” with no fresh outreach alert. This does not mark the service completed or reset its cadence. A canceled replacement may restore eligibility after a current-state check; preserve cycle/delivery receipts.
- A newer completed matching Job supersedes the old due cycle and starts the new completion-based interval. Dismissed cycles stay dismissed until a genuinely new completion cycle; no repeat notification just because a day passed or the app reopened.
- Due customers remain in the list until resolved, superseded or suppressed. Notifications contain newly due/snoozed-now items rather than repeatedly counting the entire unresolved backlog. Reading removes the notification from the existing UI feed, not the due-list record.
- In-app/email go to the selected teammate, not the customer. No company-name suffix or implied automatic customer contact. Current recipient access, email verification and applicable personal notification preferences are checked again before delivery.
- Several customers due together use one bounded digest per workflow/contact/local delivery date. Avoid one email per customer. Counts record the authorized batch found when the reminder step runs; links open current facts and may show fewer eligible customers later.

## Lifecycle and permissions

Use current Customers/Jobs read authority, Workflows author/publish/manage permissions and the existing full-workspace recipient checks. The source Job, linked customer, service and completion cycle must remain in the same workspace. No customer record existence, counts or contact details are inferred from names or inaccessible references.

Pause holds delivery and disables due-list resolution actions as appropriate. Resume uses the next configured delivery time for customers who are still eligible and have not already been notified in this cycle; it does not emit one notification for every missed day. Delete cancels pending work and blocks new resolution writes while retaining limited history. A previously sent email is not recalled. Editing interval/time/recipient/channels rechecks current cycles from their original completion anchors, preserves resolutions, and cancels superseded unsent email. Historical inclusion is an explicit one-time choice, not an incidental effect of editing.

## Architecture and efficiency

Committed Job completion/reopen events maintain a compact customer/service latest-completion projection and durable occurrence facts. Customer/archive, service/archive and matching Job schedule/status changes invalidate only affected customer/service occurrences. Never run a query per customer on page load or repeatedly scan all Jobs for each workflow.

Use the existing transactional outbox and BullMQ delayed jobs, one bounded daily digest coordination entry per workflow/local delivery date and deduplicated cycle/recipient delivery receipts. Queue workers check indexed due rows; they do not enumerate all clients/workflows on a timer. Relevant source changes update only their indexed occurrences. Execution, suppression, retries, email and list resolution use zero model calls.

Batch customer/Job/service/current-completion eligibility reads in the current authorized transaction. Use a single scoped React Query projection for the due list and global paging, cached setup choices, local form edits and scoped invalidation. Do not authenticate per row, on every keystroke, or once per possible completion date. Reuse canonical customer and Job services; no inferred customer identity from email/name/address.

Snooze/dismiss operations bind actor, workflow/version, occurrence/cycle version and request ID. Current eligibility, lifecycle and permissions are rechecked under the existing workspace mutation lock. State, operation receipt and any replacement scheduled work commit atomically. Retries cannot resolve a newer cycle or duplicate a digest.

## Proposed addition: service email drafting and reviewed sending

Requested November 22, before UI implementation. Extend the due-list flow with optional, explicitly reviewed customer outreach. This supersedes the earlier first-slice exclusion of all customer email: automatic customer sending remains excluded. This is a new customer-outreach capability, not an existing estimate-sharing action.

Each workflow's due list is already scoped to one service. Place a compact Prepare service email action above its customer table. It opens a scoped assistant side panel with the selected service and current approved template, without sending the entire customer list to the model. The user can ask for a friendly reminder, change tone or shorten it. The assistant prepares subject/body once per service; it never independently invents prices, discounts, guarantees, customer availability, scheduling links or reply capabilities.

Save draft retains a versioned workspace/service template. Saving or publishing the workflow does not send customer email or authorize automatic future outreach. Keep the draft editor and customer list visible together, and show an explicitly labeled sample preview with only supported placeholders, such as customer first name, service name and recorded completion/due date. Resolve placeholders deterministically from current authorized facts; use a neutral greeting when a personal name cannot be established. Never put internal Job descriptions, notes, staffing facts or other customers into the model prompt or outgoing template.

A customer row can offer Review email for that person. A list-level Review emails action can prepare the same saved template for an explicitly selected set of customers. Default selection is empty. Before Send emails, show the exact eligible recipients and count, sender/reply address, subject and personalized previews, with an optional per-recipient text edit. Individual emails have one recipient each; no shared To/CC list. Changing text or recipients requires the updated review to be visible. Chat confirmation does not send.

The recipient review excludes customers without a usable email, customers opted out of this outreach, archived records, already booked/open matching work and cycles already sent or queued. Show concise exclusion reasons; do not silently skip and claim everyone was contacted. Recheck address, outreach preferences, current cycle/state, template version, permissions and selected recipient set at final confirmation and again at delivery. Provide refresh/review when material facts change rather than silently changing recipients or message content.

Sending uses the existing email transport and transactional outbox/BullMQ patterns, with a new actor/review-bound send receipt and per-customer/service/completion-cycle suppression. Persist the reviewed final subject/body and one delivery intent per recipient before transport. Retry failed delivery using the same receipt; do not generate text again or resend a successful recipient. Show Queued, Sent and Failed accurately. Sent indicates provider acceptance; do not claim delivery/read/reply evidence without an implemented provider event. A sent reminder resolves outreach for this cycle but does not complete a Job, book work or change the customer's service history. Failed delivery remains actionable, and a new completion creates a genuinely new cycle.

The app has customer email fields and a shared email transport used for existing app messages, but it does not yet have a general service-outreach campaign, customer opt-out model/unsubscribe flow or reviewed service-email send endpoint. Those are required parts of this addition. Show the actual configured sender and business reply address; do not imply a custom workspace sending domain or connected mailbox unless implemented and verified. Customer-outreach permission is separate from merely publishing an internal workflow. Archive, retention and suppression must remain effective across new templates and workflows.

Efficiency: one model draft/refinement per service template, not one model call per customer. Populate previews with deterministic merge fields. Load and validate recipients in bounded batches in the existing authorized transaction, page review results with the global table/pagination, and reuse scoped React Query data. Sending, retries, opt-out suppression and status updates use zero model calls. New recipients are never added to a confirmed send batch automatically.

UI checkpoint should now include the compact due list with the service-email action, scoped drafting panel, saved subject/body, individual preview, selected-recipient review, exclusions, pending/error states and send results. No customer outreach code, provider configuration or external messages have been executed at this behavior checkpoint.
